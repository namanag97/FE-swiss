## 1.1 Alex Hormozi's Value Equation

**Formula:**

```
Value = (Dream Outcome x Perceived Likelihood of Achievement) / (Time Delay x Effort & Sacrifice)
```

The numerator "adds value." The denominator "removes value." To maximize perceived value, you maximize the top and minimize the bottom.

### The Four Variables

| Variable | Definition | Enterprise B2B Application |
|----------|-----------|---------------------------|
| **Dream Outcome** | The ideal result the buyer envisions | "Complete visibility into every process, every bottleneck, every dollar leaked" |
| **Perceived Likelihood** | How probable the buyer believes achievement is | Case studies, ROI guarantees, pilot results, Forrester TEI data |
| **Time Delay** | How long until value is realized | Celonis = 3-6 months. Sancalana target = days/weeks. This is the killer variable. |
| **Effort & Sacrifice** | What the buyer must invest (time, resources, risk, politics) | Implementation effort, data engineering dependency, internal change management |

### Applied to Process Mining

**Celonis's current equation (from buyer's perspective):**
```
Value = (Enterprise-wide process optimization x High — they're the market leader)
        / (3-6 months implementation x Massive — $1.5-5M Year 1, 15-20 FTE CoE, consultants)
```

**Sancalana's target equation:**
```
Value = (Process visibility & quick wins x Moderate — we're new, but pilot proves it)
        / (Days/weeks x Minimal — self-serve, no consultants, upload data and go)
```

### Key Insight for Enterprise B2B

Hormozi's framework works best in short sales cycles and transactional environments. For enterprise B2B with 6-month onboarding windows and long-term integration, the denominator variables (Time Delay, Effort) become disproportionately important. This is where startups win — not by promising bigger outcomes (the incumbent always wins that game) but by radically reducing the denominator.

**The strategic implication:** Don't try to out-promise Celonis on Dream Outcome. Instead, make Time Delay and Effort so low that the equation tips dramatically in your favor even with a more modest numerator.

**Limitations:** Enterprise buyers are sophisticated. They discount Dream Outcome claims heavily unless backed by verifiable data. Perceived Likelihood in enterprise deals is driven by: (1) reference customers in their industry, (2) analyst validation, (3) pilot results with their own data. A startup can't shortcut these.

## 1.2 Bain & Company's B2B Elements of Value

Bain identified **40 distinct "elements of value"** in B2B purchasing, organized into a five-level pyramid. Published in Harvard Business Review (March 2018). This is the most rigorous empirical framework for understanding what B2B buyers actually value.

### The Pyramid (Bottom to Top)

**Level 1: Table Stakes (Must-Have)**
- Meeting specifications
- Acceptable price
- Regulatory compliance
- Ethical standards

Without these, you are not in the conversation.

**Level 2: Functional Value (Economic/Product Performance)**
- Cost reduction
- Revenue growth
- Scalability
- Product quality
- Innovation
- Time savings
- Reduced effort
- Information availability
- Transparency
- Variety

**Level 3: Ease of Doing Business (Operational)**
- Time savings
- Reduced effort
- Decreased hassles
- Organization
- Simplification
- Connection
- Integration
- Responsiveness
- Expertise
- Commitment
- Stability
- Cultural fit

**Level 4: Individual Value (Personal/Career)**
- Network expansion
- Marketability
- Reputability
- Design & aesthetics
- Growth & development
- Reduced anxiety
- Fun & perks

**Level 5: Inspirational Value (Purpose/Vision)**
- Vision
- Hope
- Social responsibility

### Key Research Finding

Buyers reported they were **twice as likely to repurchase** from companies that scored high on 6 or more elements compared to companies with no high-scoring elements. The more elements you deliver on, the stronger the relationship.

### Application to Process Mining

| Bain Level | What Sancalana Must Deliver | Priority |
|-----------|---------------------------|----------|
| **Table Stakes** | Works with their ERP data, accurate results, compliant, fair pricing | Non-negotiable |
| **Functional** | Quantified cost reduction, time savings, revenue protection | Core value prop |
| **Ease of Doing Business** | Fast setup, simple UX, responsive support, integrates with existing stack | Key differentiator vs Celonis |
| **Individual** | Makes the champion look good, reduces their anxiety about the decision, career-enhancing | Often overlooked |
| **Inspirational** | "Transform your organization's process intelligence" — vision for what's possible | For executive sponsors |

### The Critical Insight

Most enterprise software companies compete only on Level 2 (Functional). They talk about ROI, cost savings, efficiency. But **buying decisions are made by humans with careers, anxieties, and aspirations**. Level 4 (Individual) elements — particularly "reduced anxiety" and "reputability" — are massively underweighted by sellers and massively influential to buyers.

For Sancalana: The champion who recommends a $200K Celonis alternative is taking a career risk. Reduce their anxiety with: (1) a free pilot with their own data, (2) a guaranteed quick win, (3) reference customers they can call. These are Level 4 value plays, not Level 2.

## 1.3 Force Management's Command of the Message

Used by **Databricks, MongoDB, Snowflake, GitLab** and most high-growth enterprise software companies. This is the dominant B2B sales methodology for complex enterprise deals.

### Core Framework

The output is a **Value Messaging Framework (VMF)** built around three components:

1. **Positive Business Outcomes (PBOs):** The measurable business results the customer wants to achieve
2. **Required Capabilities:** The product/service capabilities needed to deliver those outcomes
3. **Metrics:** Quantifiable measures proving the capabilities deliver the outcomes

### The Discovery Flow

```
Negative Consequences → Required Capabilities → Positive Business Outcomes → Metrics
(What's the pain?)     (What do you need?)      (What does success look like?)  (How will you measure?)
```

### GitLab's Implementation (Public Example)

GitLab published their entire Command of the Message framework publicly. Their three value drivers:

| Value Driver | Positive Business Outcome | Metrics |
|-------------|--------------------------|---------|
| Increase Operational Efficiencies | Simplify toolchain, reduce TCO | Developer productivity, tool consolidation |
| Deliver Better Products Faster | Accelerate delivery to meet business objectives | Cycle time, deployment frequency |
| Reduce Security & Compliance Risk | Comply with regulations without compromising speed | Vulnerabilities detected, audit pass rate |

### Applied to Process Mining (Sancalana VMF Draft)

| Value Driver | Negative Consequences (Pain) | Required Capabilities | Positive Business Outcomes | Metrics |
|-------------|-------|---------|----------|---------|
| **Operational Efficiency** | "We don't know where our process bottlenecks are. We're flying blind." | Process discovery, variant analysis, bottleneck identification | Quantified efficiency gains, reduced cycle times | % cycle time reduction, cost per transaction |
| **Cash Flow Optimization** | "We're missing early payment discounts and paying duplicate invoices." | Cash discount monitoring, duplicate detection, working capital analytics | Captured discounts, prevented leakage, freed working capital | $ cash discounts captured, $ duplicates prevented, DSO/DPO change |
| **Migration De-risking** | "Our S/4HANA migration is behind schedule and over budget." | Pre-migration process assessment, post-migration conformance | On-time, on-budget migration with quantified process baselines | Migration timeline adherence, process conformance rate |

### Why This Framework Matters

Command of the Message addresses modern buying complexity through disciplined discovery that:
- Uncovers **Negative Consequences** (quantifiable pain)
- Defines **Positive Business Outcomes** (measurable improvements)
- Aligns **Required Capabilities** to your competitive differentiation

This is exactly what complex buying committees need to justify discretionary spending and overcome status quo bias.

## 1.4 MEDDIC / MEDDPICC Value Quantification

MEDDPICC is the dominant **deal qualification** framework for enterprise software. Originated at PTC in the 1990s. Used by Salesforce, HubSpot, and virtually every enterprise SaaS company.

### The Eight Components

| Letter | Component | Definition | Value Quantification Role |
|--------|-----------|-----------|--------------------------|
| **M** | Metrics | Quantifiable measures of value | THE value quantification element — what does success look like in numbers? |
| **E** | Economic Buyer | Person with P&L authority to approve | Who signs the check? What metrics do THEY care about? |
| **D** | Decision Criteria | How the buyer evaluates options | What capabilities must you demonstrate? |
| **D** | Decision Process | Steps the buyer follows to purchase | How long, who's involved, what approvals? |
| **P** | Paper Process | Legal/procurement workflow | MSA, security review, procurement timelines |
| **I** | Identify Pain | The urgent business problems | What hurts today? What's the cost of inaction? |
| **C** | Champion | Internal advocate who sells for you | Who benefits personally from your success? |
| **C** | Competition | Alternatives being evaluated | Who else is in the deal? What's status quo? |

### Metrics: The Value Quantification Engine

Good MEDDPICC metrics are:
- **Quantified:** "$X saved" not "improved efficiency"
- **Attributed:** Directly tied to your solution
- **Time-bound:** "Within Y months"
- **Verified:** Customer agrees this is achievable

**Example for Process Mining:**

| Metric Type | Example | Evidence Source |
|------------|---------|----------------|
| Revenue protection | "$3.3M additional revenue from removing order blocks" | Forrester TEI for Celonis |
| Cost reduction | "57% reduction in P2P cycle time" | Tech Data case study |
| Cash flow | "$1.3B increase in free cash flow" | GE Healthcare case study |
| Efficiency | "95% automated invoice processing" | Tech Data case study |
| Risk reduction | "1,000 incorrect payments prevented per week" | GE Healthcare case study |

### How Enterprise Buyers Use MEDDPICC Against You

Sophisticated enterprise buyers (especially procurement teams) use MEDDPICC-like frameworks internally to evaluate vendors. They will:
1. Ask you to quantify metrics — if you can't, you lose credibility
2. Test your understanding of their decision process — if you don't know, you're not a serious vendor
3. Verify your champion's authority — a champion without budget authority is worthless
4. Compare your metrics against competition — your numbers must be credible relative to alternatives

## 1.5 The Challenger Sale Value Framework

Based on CEB/Gartner research across thousands of B2B sales interactions. **Challengers make up 40% of top-performing sales reps** and 54% of high performers in complex B2B sales.

### Three Pillars: Teach, Tailor, Take Control

| Pillar | What It Means | Application |
|--------|--------------|-------------|
| **Teach** | Deliver a disruptive insight that reframes the customer's perspective | "You think your process is 80% efficient. Our analysis of companies like yours shows it's actually 45%." |
| **Tailor** | Customize the insight for each stakeholder's specific goals | CFO sees cash flow impact. VP Ops sees cycle time. CIO sees integration simplification. |
| **Take Control** | Confidently guide the buying process | "Based on what we've seen, here's the pilot structure that will prove value in 3 weeks." |

### The Commercial Teaching Choreography (6 Steps)

This is the core Challenger methodology — a repeatable framework for creating urgency:

| Step | What Happens | Process Mining Example |
|------|-------------|----------------------|
| **1. Warmer** | Connect to a known issue the customer acknowledges | "Most companies going through S/4HANA migration discover their processes are more complex than documented." |
| **2. Reframe** | Challenge their assumption with a new perspective | "But the real risk isn't complexity — it's that 70% of companies don't discover process deviations until post-migration, when fixing them costs 5-10x more." |
| **3. Rational Drowning** | Stack data that quantifies the problem they didn't know they had | "Companies with $5B+ revenue typically have 5,000-50,000 process variants in O2C alone. Each unnecessary variant adds $X in cost." |
| **4. Emotional Impact** | Make it personal — how does this affect THEM? | "The VP Ops who owned the last failed migration at [competitor] was replaced within 6 months." |
| **5. A New Way** | Describe the solution approach (NOT your product yet) | "What if you could see every process variant, every bottleneck, every compliance gap BEFORE the migration — in days, not months?" |
| **6. Your Solution** | Now connect your product to the new way | "That's exactly what Sancalana does. Upload your event logs, get a complete process map in hours." |

### Why Challenger Works for Startups Against Incumbents

**53% of B2B loyalty** comes from the sales experience itself — specifically, the insight-led experience where reps teach customers something new about their business. This matters because:

1. Celonis's sales reps sell from a position of authority (market leader, 2,500 customers). They default to relationship selling.
2. A startup can't out-relationship Celonis. But you CAN out-teach them.
3. If you bring genuine insight about the customer's specific process problems — insight they haven't heard from Celonis — you differentiate on the sales experience itself.

**The Challenger reframe for Sancalana:** "Celonis tells you process mining takes 3-6 months to implement. That's not a feature — that's a bug. The reason it takes that long is architectural: they were built for a world of on-prem data extraction. In a world where your data is already in a cloud warehouse, the bottleneck Celonis 'solves' is one they created."

## 1.6 Framework Comparison: When to Use What

| Framework | Best For | Strength | Weakness |
|-----------|----------|----------|----------|
| **Hormozi Value Equation** | Pricing strategy, offer design, marketing messaging | Simple, intuitive, focuses on buyer perception | Too simplistic for complex enterprise deals |
| **Bain Elements of Value** | Understanding what buyers ACTUALLY value beyond ROI | Research-backed, comprehensive, includes emotional/career factors | Diagnostic, not prescriptive — tells you what matters, not what to do |
| **Command of the Message** | Sales team alignment, discovery conversations, deal execution | Practical, actionable, used by best enterprise sales orgs | Requires organizational investment to implement |
| **MEDDPICC** | Deal qualification, pipeline management, forecast accuracy | Forces rigor on every deal, identifies gaps early | Can become checkbox exercise if not culturally embedded |
| **Challenger Sale** | Early-stage selling against incumbents, thought leadership | Perfect for startups who can teach but can't out-resource | Requires deep domain expertise to generate genuine insights |

**Recommendation for Sancalana:**
- **Marketing & positioning:** Hormozi (minimize denominator — time/effort) + Bain (address all five levels)
- **Sales discovery:** Command of the Message (PBOs + Required Capabilities + Metrics)
- **Deal qualification:** MEDDPICC (especially Metrics, Economic Buyer, Champion)
- **Competitive selling:** Challenger (reframe implementation time, teach buyers about modern architecture)

## 2.1 The Value Spectrum

Enterprise value falls on a spectrum from hard (directly measurable on P&L) to soft (real but harder to quantify):

```
HARD ◄─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────► SOFT

Direct Cost      Cost          Revenue        Risk         Strategic      Productivity    Employee
Savings          Avoidance     Protection     Reduction    Advantage      Gains           Experience

$ on invoice     $ not spent   $ not lost     $ exposure   Competitive    Hours saved     Satisfaction
                                              reduced      positioning                    Reduced anxiety

CFO loves ←──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────→ CFO skeptical
```

## 2.2 Definitions and Examples

### Hard Savings (Direct Cost Reduction)

Hard savings are directly measurable, show up on P&L, and have invoices/receipts as proof.

| Category | Process Mining Example | Typical Range |
|----------|----------------------|---------------|
| Cash discount capture | Pay invoices early to capture 2% discounts | 1-3% of addressable spend |
| Duplicate payment prevention | Stop paying the same invoice twice | 0.1-0.5% of AP spend |
| Headcount reallocation | Automate manual invoice processing | 20-50% FTE reduction in specific functions |
| Vendor cost reduction | Identify maverick spend, enforce contract pricing | 2-5% of off-contract spend |
| Penalty avoidance | Prevent late payment penalties | Direct contractual amounts |

**Why CFOs trust hard savings:** There is an invoice, payroll stub, bill, receipt, or the like associated with the expense. No ambiguity about the amount saved.

### Cost Avoidance (Soft Savings Tier 1)

Real financial benefits, but harder to trace to a specific line item.

| Category | Process Mining Example | Typical Range |
|----------|----------------------|---------------|
| Cycle time reduction | Faster invoice processing = less working capital tied up | Opportunity cost of capital x days saved |
| Error prevention | Fewer rework loops = less wasted time | Cost per error x error rate reduction |
| Compliance improvement | Fewer audit findings = less remediation cost | Audit remediation cost avoided |
| Infrastructure savings | Consolidating/optimizing systems revealed by process mining | System license and maintenance costs |

### Revenue Protection / Growth (Soft Savings Tier 2)

| Category | Process Mining Example | Typical Range |
|----------|----------------------|---------------|
| DSO reduction | Collect receivables faster = better cash flow | Working capital freed x cost of capital |
| Order block removal | Unblock orders faster = recognize revenue sooner | Revenue acceleration (Celonis: $3.3M over 3 years) |
| Customer retention | Better order fulfillment = fewer lost customers | Revenue at risk from churn |
| Upsell identification | Process data reveals expansion opportunities | Revenue opportunity identified |

### Risk Reduction (Soft Savings Tier 3)

| Category | Process Mining Example | Typical Range |
|----------|----------------------|---------------|
| Fraud detection | Identify suspicious payment patterns | Exposure reduction (probability x impact) |
| Regulatory compliance | Continuous monitoring vs. periodic audits | Fine avoidance + audit cost reduction |
| Migration de-risking | Process discovery before S/4HANA migration | Cost overrun prevention (typically 50-100% of project budget) |
| Operational resilience | Identify single points of failure in processes | Business continuity value |

### Strategic / Productivity Value (Soft Savings Tier 4)

| Category | Process Mining Example | Measurement Challenge |
|----------|----------------------|----------------------|
| Decision quality | Better process data = better decisions | Nearly impossible to quantify directly |
| Employee satisfaction | Less manual drudgery, more meaningful work | Measurable via surveys but hard to monetize |
| Organizational agility | Faster process changes, quicker response to market | Real but abstract |
| Competitive intelligence | Benchmark processes against industry peers | Informational value, not financial |

## 2.3 How Enterprise Buyers Evaluate Each Type

### The CFO's Hierarchy

```
What CFOs APPROVE without debate:
├── Hard savings with >12-month track record (proven cost reduction)
├── Hard savings with pilot evidence (demonstrated in POC)
│
What CFOs APPROVE with business case:
├── Cost avoidance with credible methodology (TCO model, benchmark data)
├── Revenue protection with historical data (DSO reduction, order block removal)
│
What CFOs are SKEPTICAL of:
├── Risk reduction (unless regulatory/compliance with specific penalties)
├── Productivity gains (without before/after measurement methodology)
│
What CFOs DISMISS:
├── Strategic value without quantification
├── "Improved visibility" without tied outcomes
└── "Better decision-making" claims
```

### The VP Operations' Hierarchy

VPs of Operations evaluate differently — they weight operational metrics more heavily:

1. **Cycle time reduction** (can they see and measure it daily?)
2. **Throughput improvement** (more processed with same resources)
3. **Quality improvement** (fewer errors, rework, returns)
4. **Compliance rate** (audit readiness, fewer findings)
5. **Team productivity** (visible in team dashboards)

### The CIO's Hierarchy

CIOs focus on technology-related value:

1. **Integration simplicity** (how does this fit our stack?)
2. **Total cost of ownership** (software + implementation + maintenance)
3. **Security and compliance** (SOC 2, data residency, access controls)
4. **Time to implement** (IT resource burden)
5. **Vendor stability** (will they exist in 3 years?)

## 2.4 The "3-Bucket" Approach for Business Cases

Structure value into three buckets that align with how CFOs think:

```
Total Value = Hard Savings + Efficiency Gains + Risk-Adjusted Strategic Value

Bucket 1: Hard Savings (80% probability weighting)
- Cash discounts captured: $X
- Duplicate payments prevented: $Y
- Penalty avoidance: $Z

Bucket 2: Efficiency Gains (50% probability weighting)
- FTE reallocation value: $A (hours saved x loaded cost)
- Cycle time improvement: $B (working capital freed x cost of capital)
- Automation uplift: $C (transactions automated x cost per manual transaction)

Bucket 3: Risk-Adjusted Strategic Value (25% probability weighting)
- Migration cost overrun prevention: $D x 25% probability
- Compliance violation avoidance: $E x probability of violation
- Revenue at risk protection: $F x probability of loss

CONSERVATIVE TOTAL = (Bucket 1 x 0.8) + (Bucket 2 x 0.5) + (Bucket 3 x 0.25)
```

This probability-weighted approach is what sophisticated CFOs expect. It shows intellectual honesty and builds trust.

## 3.1 What CFOs Expect for a $100K-$500K Purchase

### The Format

CFOs expect a business case with these sections:

1. **Executive Summary** (1 page)
   - Problem statement with quantified pain
   - Proposed solution with projected ROI
   - Investment required and payback period

2. **Current State Analysis** (2-3 pages)
   - Quantified baseline of current process performance
   - Cost of status quo (inaction cost)
   - Benchmarks vs. industry peers

3. **Financial Analysis** (3-5 pages)
   - NPV (Net Present Value)
   - IRR (Internal Rate of Return)
   - Payback period
   - TCO over 3-5 years
   - Sensitivity analysis

4. **Risk Assessment** (1-2 pages)
   - Implementation risk
   - Vendor risk
   - Change management risk
   - Mitigation strategies

5. **Implementation Plan** (1-2 pages)
   - Timeline and milestones
   - Resource requirements
   - Quick wins timeline (first 90 days)

### The Key Financial Metrics

**Net Present Value (NPV):**
```
NPV = Σ (Cash Flow_t / (1 + discount_rate)^t) - Initial Investment

Where:
- Cash Flow_t = net benefit in year t (benefits - costs)
- discount_rate = company's WACC or hurdle rate (typically 8-15% for enterprises)
- t = year (0, 1, 2, 3...)

Rule: NPV > 0 means the investment creates value
```

**Internal Rate of Return (IRR):**
```
IRR = the discount rate at which NPV = 0

Rule: IRR > hurdle rate means the investment is worthwhile
Typical enterprise hurdle rate: 10-15%
For a $100K-$500K software investment, IRR should be >25% to justify the decision effort
```

**Payback Period:**
```
Simple Payback = Total Investment / Annual Net Benefit

CFO expectations by investment size:
- $100K: Payback < 6 months
- $250K: Payback < 9 months
- $500K: Payback < 12 months

Discounted Payback = time until cumulative discounted cash flows > initial investment
```

**Total Cost of Ownership (3-Year):**
```
TCO = Year 1 (License + Implementation + Training + Internal Resources)
    + Year 2 (License + Maintenance + Support + Internal Resources)
    + Year 3 (License + Maintenance + Support + Internal Resources)
    + Opportunity Cost (resources diverted from other projects)
```

|----------|-------------------|-----------------|-----|-----|---------|
| **Base Case** | 100% of projected | 100% of projected | $X | Y% | Z months |
| **Conservative** | 60% of projected | 120% of projected | $A | B% | C months |
| **Pessimistic** | 40% of projected | 150% of projected | $D | E% | F months |
| **Optimistic** | 130% of projected | 90% of projected | $G | H% | I months |

**The rule:** Even the pessimistic scenario should show positive NPV and payback < 18 months. If it doesn't, the business case isn't strong enough.

## 3.4 What Makes CFOs Say Yes vs. No

**CFOs approve when:**
- The payback period is under 12 months (ideally under 6)
- Hard savings alone justify the investment (soft savings are bonus)
- There's a clear, staged rollout (pilot → department → enterprise)
- Risk is contained (no multi-year lock-in, can stop if it doesn't work)
- They can see the measurement methodology (how will we KNOW we got ROI?)
- Comparable customers have verified results (not just vendor claims)

**CFOs reject when:**
- Value is described in vague terms ("improved visibility," "better insights")
- No credible baseline exists (how do you know current state?)
- Benefits depend on organizational change that hasn't been committed to
- ROI timeline extends beyond 18 months for this investment size
- The vendor can't explain what happens if it doesn't work

# PART 4: VALUE WATERFALL / VALUE TREE METHODOLOGY

---

## 4.1 What Is a Value Driver Tree?

A Value Driver Tree (VDT) is a hierarchical decomposition that breaks down a top-level business metric (revenue, EBITDA, free cash flow, ROIC) into its component parts, allowing you to identify which operational levers drive value.

### Core Structure

```
Root Metric (e.g., Free Cash Flow)
├── Revenue
│   ├── Price × Volume × Mix
│   ├── Customer Count × ARPA
│   └── New Revenue + Expansion - Churn
├── COGS
│   ├── Infrastructure
│   ├── Support
│   └── Professional Services
├── Operating Expenses
│   ├── R&D
│   ├── S&M
│   └── G&A
└── Working Capital
    ├── Accounts Receivable (DSO)
    ├── Inventory (DIO)
    └── Accounts Payable (DPO)
```

### Mathematical Relationships

| Relationship | Formula | Example |
|-------------|---------|---------|
| **Additive** | Total = A + B + C | Total Cost = Fixed + Variable + Overhead |
| **Multiplicative** | Result = A × B | Revenue = Price × Volume |
| **Ratio** | Metric = A / B | Working Capital = COGS / Inventory Turns |
| **Subtractive** | Net = A - B | Profit = Revenue - Cost |

## 4.2 Value Waterfall for Process Mining

A value waterfall shows how total value "flows" from identified opportunity to realized benefit, with each step reducing the value through implementation factors:

```
TOTAL ADDRESSABLE VALUE (100%)
│
├── Step 1: Process Analysis Identifies Opportunities
│   └── Typical: 60-80% of addressable value identified
│
├── Step 2: Prioritize by Impact & Feasibility
│   └── Typical: Top 30-50% of identified value is actionable
│
├── Step 3: Implement Fixes
│   └── Typical: 70-90% implementation success rate
│
├── Step 4: Sustain Improvements
│   └── Typical: 60-80% of improvements sustained after 12 months
│
└── REALIZED VALUE = 100% × 70% × 40% × 80% × 70% = ~16% of total addressable
```

**This is why process mining vendors report "identified" vs "realized" value:**
- Celonis reports $18B+ "identified" but $8.1B "realized" — roughly 45% conversion
- The gap between identified and realized is the implementation and sustenance challenge

## 4.4 How to Build a Customer-Specific Value Tree

### Step 1: Identify the Root Metric
Ask: "What financial metric does your CFO/COO report on that this project could improve?"
Common answers: Working capital, cost per transaction, cycle time, compliance rate, revenue leakage

### Step 2: Decompose Into Drivers
Work backwards from the root metric to operational KPIs that process mining can measure.

### Step 3: Baseline Each Driver
Use the customer's actual data to establish current state. This is where process mining itself becomes the diagnostic tool — you can offer the baseline analysis as part of the sales process.

### Step 4: Set Targets Using Benchmarks
Reference industry benchmarks and comparable customer results.

### Step 5: Calculate Value Per Driver
Multiply the improvement delta by the monetary conversion factor.

### Step 6: Aggregate and Risk-Adjust
Sum all driver values, apply probability weights, present the waterfall.

## 5.1 Time-to-Value: Definition and Measurement

**Time to Value (TTV)** is the time between when a customer starts using a product and when they achieve their first meaningful outcome.

### Types of TTV

| Type | Definition | Process Mining Example |
|------|-----------|----------------------|
| **Time to Basic Value** | First useful output | See a process map from their data |
| **Time to Aha Moment** | First surprising insight | "We didn't know we had 5,000 process variants" |
| **Time to Measurable Value** | First quantified benefit | "$X in duplicate payments identified" |
| **Time to Exceeded Expectations** | Outcome surpasses what was promised | ROI exceeds the business case projection |

### SaaS Benchmark

According to benchmark data, the average TTV in SaaS is approximately **1 day, 12 hours, and 23 minutes**. This is for all SaaS — enterprise tools may be longer, but the trend is toward faster.

## 5.2 The Implementation Speed Gap

### Current Market Reality

| Solution | Time to Basic Value | Time to Measurable Value | Time to Full Deployment |
|----------|-------------------|-------------------------|------------------------|
| **Celonis** | 4-8 weeks | 3-6 months | 12-24 months |

### Why Celonis Is Slow

Celonis's architecture was built in the 2010s for a world where:
- Data lived in on-prem ERPs (SAP ECC, Oracle EBS)
- Extraction required custom ABAP programs or ETL pipelines
- Data transformation was a project in itself
- Implementation required specialized Celonis consultants
- Each process required a custom data model

**What's changed:**
- Cloud data warehouses (Snowflake, Databricks, BigQuery) are ubiquitous
- Data is increasingly already extracted, transformed, and centralized
- Modern architectures can query warehouse data directly
- LLMs can automate data modeling and process interpretation
- Self-serve products have proven viable in comparable enterprise categories

### The 10x Speed Advantage

If Celonis takes **3-6 months** to first measurable value and Sancalana can do it in **days/weeks**, the Hormozi equation shifts dramatically:

```
Celonis Value  = (High Outcome × High Likelihood) / (6 months × Massive Effort)
Sancalana Value = (Moderate Outcome × Moderate Likelihood) / (2 weeks × Minimal Effort)

Even if Sancalana's numerator is 50% of Celonis,
the denominator advantage makes total perceived value HIGHER
if Time Delay drops from 180 days to 14 days (12.8x)
and Effort drops from $1.5M to $50K (30x)
```

| Multi-year willingness | Common (3 years) | Reluctant (prefer annual) | Offer multi-year discounts of 25-35% |
| Free trial expectation | 14-day standard | 21-30 days expected; "try before buy" is cultural norm | Longer trial periods needed |
| Procurement process | Structured, 3-6 months | Relationship-driven, 6-12 months for large enterprises; PSUs even longer | Patience required |
| Price-to-value perception | "Software is an investment" | "Software should be priced like a utility" | Frame pricing around value delivered, not cost |

### Indian Enterprise Buying Committee Structure

Indian enterprise technology purchases involve **25-33 stakeholders** on average. 44% of software decisions are made by committee; this rises to 71% in large enterprises.

```
Typical Indian Enterprise Decision Flow:

Business Owner → IT Team → Procurement → Finance → Management Committee
(identifies      (evaluates   (negotiates  (approves    (final sign-off
 the need)        tech fit)     price)      budget)      for large deals)
```

**Key differences from Western buying:**
- **Trust-building requires relationship investment** — cold outreach is less effective; warm introductions and local partnerships matter more
- **Government validation carries weight** — certifications, PSU customers, compliance attestations influence private sector decisions
- **Hierarchy matters** — a recommendation from a senior leader at a peer company carries enormous weight
- **Total cost obsession** — Indian CFOs scrutinize implementation costs, hidden fees, and ongoing maintenance more aggressively
- **Services bundling expected** — preference for implementation support bundled with software, not separate
